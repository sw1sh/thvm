FindProof 0.1

Nik Murzin
Wolfram Institute, USA

Overview

FindProof 0.1 is a checker for TSTP first-order proofs, entered in ProoVer 2026. Checking is treated as proof replay: a proof is parsed into its sequence of annotated formulae, and each derived step is validated independently by dispatching on the inference rule that introduced it. A single SZS verdict is then reported for the whole proof, one of Verified, FailedVerified, or NotVerified. The checker is deliberately conservative. A step whose validity cannot be established is reported as NotVerified rather than guessed, so that an unsound proof is never accepted; soundness of the checker is favoured over coverage, in keeping with the scoring, which penalises accepting a buggy proof far more heavily than abstaining.

Implementation

FindProof is a single self-contained C binary with no external dependencies. The same binary is the FindProof equational prover; proof checking is its --check mode. There is no Wolfram kernel and no external prover. Formulae are read from the TSTP source by a hand-written recursive-descent parser, covering the full first-order form syntax, the quantifiers, the connectives, and equality, directly into the first-order term representation of the reasoning core. The per-rule obligations are then discharged against that core, which is the same clausal saturation prover, resolution together with superposition and paramodulation for equality, that the equational engine is built on, so entailment questions are answered by the prover itself rather than by a separate decision procedure.

For an instantiate step the conclusion must be a substitution instance of a parent, checked by first-order matching and, failing that, by entailment. For a negated_conjecture step the conclusion must be the logical negation of the parent: the two are required to entail each other, each direction decided by the saturation prover, so that a wrong quantifier dual, a universal negated as a universal rather than an existential, is caught soundly rather than by syntactic comparison alone. For a skolemize step a genuinely fresh Skolem symbol must be introduced and must occur in the resulting formula, a structural equisatisfiability check, since Skolemisation is not a logical consequence. For a consequence, horn, or deduction step the conclusion must be entailed by the parents: the parents and the negated conclusion are clausified together and the prover is run to saturation. A derived empty clause certifies the entailment and the step is accepted; a saturated clause set with no empty clause exhibits a counter-model and the step is rejected as unsound; a search that does not settle within the resource bound leaves the step undecided. The refutation as a whole must close with $false.

A verdict of FailedVerified is emitted only when a step is certainly unsound, and the offending step is named. Any step that cannot be settled, an inference rule outside the repertoire or an entailment the bounded saturation neither proves nor refutes, yields NotVerified for the whole proof. The entire checking pass is resource-bounded, so a saturation whose equality reasoning outgrows the term store unwinds to a clean NotVerified: every proof receives exactly one SZS verdict, and the conservative fallback is always to abstain, never to accept a proof that has not been confirmed.

Expected Competition Performance

On the provided examples the checker classifies the three valid proofs as Verified. Of the four buggy proofs it confirms and localises three: a universal conjecture negated as a universal rather than an existential, the reuse of an already-introduced Skolem symbol, and a declared Skolem symbol that is absent from the resulting formula. The fourth buggy proof draws a single disjunct from an equational disjunction by deduction; establishing that this is not entailed requires more than the bounded saturation certifies, so the checker reports NotVerified and abstains rather than guess. On the hidden set the checker is sound by construction, it never accepts an unsound proof and never reports a valid proof as buggy, and it abstains on proofs whose steps it cannot settle, in keeping with the scoring, which penalises a wrong verdict far more heavily than abstaining.
