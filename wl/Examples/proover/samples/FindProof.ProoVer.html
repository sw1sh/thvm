Nik Murzin
Wolfram Institute, USA

Architecture

FindProof is an equational theorem prover based on unfailing completion; the same binary is entered in the CASC unit-equality division. As a proof checker it validates a first-order TSTP proof by replay, discharging each derived step independently by dispatching on the inference rule that introduced it and reporting one SZS status for the whole proof [Sys1]. An instantiate step is required to be a substitution instance of a parent; a negated_conjecture step to be the negation of its parent, checked by entailment in both directions so that a wrong quantifier dual is caught; a skolemize step to introduce a fresh Skolem symbol that occurs in the result; and a consequence, horn, or deduction step to be entailed by its parents. Entailment is decided by the system's clausal saturation calculus, resolution with superposition and paramodulation for equality [Lib1]: the parents and the negated conclusion are clausified and saturated, and a derived empty clause certifies the step. The proof as a whole must close with the empty clause.

Strategies

Checking is conservative, following the scoring, under which a wrong verdict costs more than abstention. A step is reported unsound only when a completed saturation exhibits a counter-model; a step whose inference rule lies outside the implemented set, or whose entailment the bounded saturation neither proves nor refutes, leaves the proof unverified rather than guessed.

Implementation

FindProof is a single self-contained C binary with no external dependencies. Proofs in TSTP syntax are read directly by a recursive-descent parser into the term representation of the reasoning core, which is the same clausal saturation engine used for equational proof search, so the checker reasons with full equality rather than treating equational atoms as opaque.

Expected Competition Performance

FindProof is entered in ProoVer 2026. It is expected to be sound, accepting no invalid proof and rejecting no valid one, and to abstain on proofs that use inference rules outside its repertoire.
