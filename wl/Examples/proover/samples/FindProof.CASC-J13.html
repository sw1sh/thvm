FindProof 0.1

Nik Murzin
Wolfram Institute, USA

Architecture

FindProof 0.1 [Sys1] is the unit-equational entry of the thvm automated reasoning system. It is an unfailing (ordered) Knuth-Bendix completion prover [Lib1]: the unit-equational axioms are saturated towards a convergent rewrite system while the negated conjecture is normalised, and a refutation is reported when the two sides of the conjecture are joined. Equations that cannot be oriented by the reduction ordering are used unfailingly, so both faces are superposed, which preserves refutational completeness. Two reduction orderings are supported, the Knuth-Bendix ordering and the lexicographic path ordering, with symbol precedences generated automatically. Critical pairs between oriented rules are the basic inferences; redundant critical pairs are discarded by ground-joinability testing, the Bachmair-Dershowitz connectedness criterion, forward and backward subsumption and demodulation, and right-hand-side interreduction. A proof object is reconstructed from the saturation trace, checked internally by replaying every rewrite step, and rendered as a TPTP CNFRefutation.

Strategies

The search is ordered by a weighted critical-pair selection heuristic over a priority queue, with a Waldmeister-style selection ratio that interleaves a first-in-first-out pick at a fixed cadence so that fairness, and hence completeness, is retained. The default strategy is problem-aware: the algebraic structure of the axioms together with the goal is recognised (group, abelian group, ring, combinatory logic, associative-commutative, and Sheffer/nand classes), after which a configuration tuned for that class is front-loaded and a fixed fallback portfolio is appended, so that the schedule never solves less than the portfolio alone. Named configurations modelled on the published defaults of Waldmeister, Vampire-UEQ, Twee, and E are available. Under a wall- clock limit the available time is divided across the configurations of the schedule. Strategy tuning is structural, being selected from the detected algebraic class of the problem, and is not tuned to individual competition problems.

Implementation

The completion engine is implemented in C, with terms represented as flatterms over a term bank, perfect-discrimination-tree indexing for rewriting and subsumption, a binary heap for the critical-pair queue, and an off-heap trace from which proofs are reconstructed. The proof search surface, the problem encoding, the proof-object reconstruction, and the TPTP/SZS rendering are implemented in the Wolfram Language as the THVMLink paclet [Sys1], and are driven through the Wolfram Engine. Input problems in TPTP syntax are parsed by the WolframParser paclet [Lib2]. The system is run as a single command per problem through wolframscript.

Expected Competition Performance

FindProof is entered in the UEQ division. As a first entry built on an unfailing-completion core with structure-aware strategy selection modelled on Waldmeister and Vampire-UEQ, it is expected to solve the standard equational classes, including Boolean and ternary Boolean algebra, group theory, ring theory, and combinatory logic, while trailing the established UEQ systems on the hardest problems that demand very long completions or more aggressive redundancy elimination. A mid-field placement is anticipated.
