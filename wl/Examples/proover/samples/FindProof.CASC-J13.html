FindProof 0.1

Nik Murzin
Wolfram Institute, USA

Architecture

FindProof is an equational theorem prover built on unfailing (ordered) Knuth-Bendix completion [Lib1], and a deliberate continuation of the Waldmeister line of provers [Lib3], which won the CASC unit-equality division at every competition in which it was ranked between 1997 and 2014 and whose development ended in the early 2000s. The completion loop, critical-pair selection, queue management, and normalisation strategy reimplement the Waldmeister design faithfully enough that, on classical algebraic problems, the default configuration reproduces Waldmeister's critical-pair selection sequence exactly; the intention is that the design should not be lost with the original implementation, and should have a maintained successor that can continue to evolve.

The axioms are saturated towards a convergent rewrite system while the goals are kept normalised, and a proof is reported when the two sides of every goal are joined; a conjecture given as several negative units is proved as a multi-goal conjunction against one saturation. Equations that cannot be oriented by the reduction ordering are used unfailingly, so both faces are superposed, which preserves refutational completeness. Two reduction orderings are supported, the Knuth-Bendix ordering and the lexicographic path ordering, with symbol precedences generated automatically. Redundant critical pairs are discarded by ground-joinability testing, the Bachmair-Dershowitz connectedness criterion, forward and backward subsumption and demodulation, and right-hand-side interreduction. Proofs are reconstructed from the recorded completion trace, with the closing rewrite chain re-derived against the final rule set, and are rendered as a TPTP CNFRefutation.

The system is not limited to unit equality by design: full first-order input is handled by equationalization, in which propositions are Skolemized and encoded as equations over a Boolean-algebra axiomatisation, proved by the same completion core, and decoded back into a predicate-logic proof. The equationalization layer currently lives in the optional Wolfram Language surface and is being moved into the core; the present entry competes in UEQ.

Strategies

The search is ordered by a weighted critical-pair selection heuristic over a priority queue, with a Waldmeister-style selection ratio that interleaves a first-in-first-out pick at a fixed cadence so that fairness, and hence completeness, is retained. The default configuration is the faithful Waldmeister strategy stack. The full strategy surface, comprising some fifty-five switches covering the reduction ordering, critical-pair weighting, weight limits, redundancy criteria, goal treatment, and queue management, is exposed as command-line options, and named configurations modelled on the published defaults of Waldmeister, Twee, and Vampire-UEQ are provided. In competition the system runs a fixed schedule of such configurations, each in its own child process under a slice of the wall-clock limit, stopping at the first proof. The schedule and its slice allocation are derived from coverage measurements on the problem sets of previous competitions; strategy selection is not tuned to individual competition problems.

Implementation

FindProof is a single self-contained C binary with no external dependencies. Terms are represented as flatterms over a term bank, rewriting and subsumption retrieval use perfect discrimination trees with Waldmeister's refinements, the critical-pair queue is a binary heap over a compact twelve-byte critical-pair encoding, and the proof trace is kept off-heap in a packed store from which proof objects are reconstructed on demand. Problems in TPTP CNF syntax are read directly, with include directives resolved against the problem directory and the TPTP environment variable; positive units are taken as axioms and negative units as goals, so the encoded problem variants that place ground facts under the negated-conjecture role are handled uniformly. Result lines follow the SZS ontology, with Satisfiable claimed only under a strategy with no completeness-breaking pruning and a ground goal.

An optional Wolfram Language layer drives the same engine in-process and adds proof objects verified by internal replay, the predicate-logic equationalization, and notebook-based proof exploration; it is a design feature of the system, not a dependency, and the competition binary is the standalone C build.

Expected Competition Performance

FindProof is entered in the UEQ division. The faithful Waldmeister default solves the standard equational classes, including Boolean and ternary Boolean algebra, group and ring theory, lattice theory, and combinatory logic, and the schedule adds coverage from ground-joinability and connectedness configurations on the associative-commutative classes where pure completion saturates slowly. It is expected to trail the leading portfolio systems, whose unit-equality schedules have benefited from years of tuning, and a mid-field placement is anticipated.
