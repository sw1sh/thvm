FindProof 0.1

N. Murzin
Wolfram Institute, USA

Architecture

FindProof [Sys1] is an equational theorem prover based on unfailing (ordered) Knuth-Bendix completion [Lib1]. It continues the Waldmeister line of provers [Lib3], which won the CASC unit-equality division at every competition in which it was ranked between 1997 and 2014; the completion loop, critical-pair selection, queue management, and normalisation reproduce the Waldmeister design, and on classical algebraic problems the default configuration reproduces Waldmeister's critical-pair selection sequence exactly.

The axioms are saturated towards a convergent rewrite system while the goals are kept normalised, and a proof is reported when the two sides of every goal are joined; a conjecture given as several negative units is proved as a multi-goal conjunction against one saturation. Equations that cannot be oriented by the reduction ordering are used unfailingly, so that both faces are superposed, which preserves refutational completeness. The Knuth-Bendix ordering and the lexicographic path ordering are supported, with symbol precedences generated automatically. Redundant critical pairs are discarded by ground-joinability testing, the connectedness criterion, forward and backward subsumption and demodulation, encompassment demodulation, and right-hand-side interreduction. Proofs are reconstructed from the recorded completion trace, the closing rewrite chain re-derived against the final rule set, and rendered as a TPTP CNFRefutation.

Full first-order input is handled by a clausal front-end that reduces FOF to the same unit-equational core: formulae are clausified (negation normal form, Skolemization, conjunctive normal form), Horn clauses are encoded as unit equations over a conditional-application operator with a goal-directed unit-release discipline, and non-Horn clauses pass through a Boolean disjunction-and-equality lane with Tseitin naming of deep disjunctions. A propositional pre-pass decides the near-propositional fragment directly, goal-directed axiom selection cuts very large axiom sets to the relevant slice, and functors beyond the term bank's arity bound are curried. The entry competes in the UEQ and FOF divisions.

Strategies

The search is ordered by a weighted critical-pair selection heuristic over a priority queue, with a Waldmeister-style selection ratio that interleaves a first-in-first-out pick at a fixed cadence, so that fairness, and hence completeness, is retained. The full strategy surface, some hundred and twenty switches covering the reduction ordering, critical-pair weighting, weight limits, redundancy criteria, goal treatment, and queue management, is exposed as command-line options. In competition a fixed portfolio of about twenty configurations races concurrently in a bounded process pool, each configuration holding the full wall-clock limit, and the first proof wins. The configurations are modelled on the published strategies of Waldmeister, Twee, and Vampire, and include goal flattening, ground-joinability with connectedness, a condensed-detachment discipline for the encoded Horn problems, an equational encoding of existential goals, encompassment demodulation, and associative-commutative completion; configurations whose trigger pattern is absent from the problem exit immediately and free their pool slot. The portfolio is derived from coverage measurements on the problem sets of previous competitions; strategy selection is not tuned to individual problems.

Implementation

FindProof is a single self-contained, statically linked C program with no external dependencies. Terms are represented as flatterms over a term bank; rewriting and subsumption retrieval use perfect discrimination trees; the critical-pair queue is a binary heap over a compact twelve-byte critical-pair encoding; and the completion trace is kept off-heap in a packed store from which proof objects are reconstructed on demand. Problems in TPTP CNF and FOF syntax [Lib2] are read directly by a recursive-descent parser, with include directives resolved against the problem directory and the TPTP environment variable. Result lines follow the SZS ontology, with Satisfiable claimed only under a strategy with no completeness-breaking pruning and a ground goal; a hard wall-clock guard, independent of the search loop, guarantees exactly one status line within the time limit. The system is available from the author.

Expected Competition Performance

FindProof is entered in the UEQ and FOF divisions. On the UEQ problem sets of the four most recent competitions, run at the competition time limit, FindProof solves within a few problems of the winning systems' official scores, and a placement close to the leaders is hoped for. In FOF, full first-order input is proved through the clausal equational encoding. On a stratified sample of problems drawn from nineteen TPTP first-order domains, run at a competition-scale time limit, about a third were solved; the encoding is effective on the Horn-like and near-propositional problems and weak on the large non-Horn axiom sets, where native superposition is much stronger. A lower-middle placement is anticipated, above the connection and instantiation systems and below the established saturation provers.
